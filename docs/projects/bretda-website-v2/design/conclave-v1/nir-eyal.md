# Nir Eyal — Conclave v1 Review: Bretda Website (stitch-v1)

> Lens: Hooked (Trigger → Action → Variable Reward → Investment) + Fogg's B=MAP
> (Behavior = Motivation × Ability × Prompt), **adapted to a high-consideration,
> high-ticket luxury purchase**. For an US$8k–24k made-to-order table, this is NOT
> a habit loop — nobody buys a sinuca table weekly. So I down-weight *Frequency*
> (my usual #1 criterion) and re-weight toward **internal trigger → desire**,
> **Ability/friction on the inquiry path**, and **Investment as commitment escalation**.
> The Hook here runs across *sessions over weeks of deliberation*, not within one visit.

---

## How I'm scoring

A luxury considered purchase still obeys B=MAP at the moment of inquiry:
- **Motivation** = desire the site manufactures (status, identity, beauty, scarcity).
- **Ability** = how frictionless the path to "Request a Quote" is.
- **Prompt (Trigger)** = whether the CTA fires when motivation peaks.
The buyer arrives carrying an **internal trigger** (the discomfort of an unfinished
trophy room / the aspiration to own something nobody else has). The site's job is to
*attach that itch to Bretda* and then make scratching it (inquiring) trivially easy.

## Screen-by-screen

### 01 — Home / Hero — **CONVERSION/DESIRE: 8/10**
The single best motivational asset in the set. Dark cinematic frame + the Paraíba-blue
gemstone table glowing as the hero = an **external trigger that lands directly on an
internal one**: "I want to be the kind of person who owns *that*." The headline
"Brazil's gemstones, made to play" is identity + scarcity + utility compressed into
one line — good. "MADE TO ORDER · BRAZIL" microtag is a low-key exclusivity cue
(this is bespoke, not stock — that *raises* desire for this audience).

Behavioral gaps:
- **Two competing prompts at the top**: "INQUIRE" (nav) and "EXPLORE THE COLLECTION"
  (hero CTA). At peak hero-desire the *primary* action is "Explore," which is correct
  for a cold considered buyer (you must build desire before asking) — but "Inquire"
  sitting passively in the nav means the warm visitor who is *already* sold has to
  hunt. Fine as-is, but see fix #1.
- **No variable reward teased.** Nothing on the hero hints that exploring will reveal
  something unpredictable and delightful (each gemstone table is visually distinct).
  A micro-line like "12 stones. No two tables alike." would prime the variable-reward
  loop before the click.

### 02 — Collection (single band) — **CONVERSION/DESIRE: 7/10**
This is where the **variable reward of the rabbit hole** lives, and the grid mostly
delivers it: 8 named tables (Paraíba, Esmeralda, Ametista, Água-marinha, Topázio
Imperial, Alexandria, Heliodoro, Morganita), each a distinct gemstone identity. The
Brazilian-gemstone naming is a strong **identity/collection mechanic** — it invites
"which stone is *mine*?" self-categorization, which is exactly the kind of variable,
self-relevant reward that pulls a deliberating buyer back across sessions.

Behavioral gaps:
- **No price on the cards.** The brief says ALL prices are exposed — honor that *here*,
  not just on the PDP. For this buyer, a visible "From US$X" is not a deterrent, it's a
  **qualifier and a trust signal** (luxury hides nothing it's proud of). Hiding it adds
  an Ability cost: forces a click to learn the most decision-relevant fact.
- **Weak per-card prompt.** "VIEW MORE" (or equivalent) is a soft, generic trigger.
  Naming the action to the object ("See Paraíba") increases motivation-to-click.
- The grid is long and uniform — **no anchoring or sequencing**. A deliberating buyer
  benefits from a "most requested" / "newest stone" flag to break decision paralysis.

### 03 — PDP / Paraíba — **CONVERSION/DESIRE: 8/10**
Strong **Action + Investment** screen. "From R$24,500" exposed (good — though for an
export-only USA/Europe site this MUST render as US$/€, see fix #2). The dual CTA
"REQUEST A QUOTE" (primary, filled) + a secondary action is the right hierarchy: one
loud prompt at the exact moment motivation peaks. The **"Why this object / Why this
house / Why now"** narrative triple is genuinely smart behavioral copy — it sequentially
dismantles the three hesitations of a high-ticket buyer (is the *object* worth it → is
the *maker* legitimate → why act *now*). "Why now" is a clean, non-manipulative
**urgency/internal-trigger reactivation** (made-to-order lead times, gemstone scarcity)
rather than a fake countdown. Keep it real and you stay on the ethical side of my
Regret Test.

Behavioral gaps:
- **The single biggest missed Investment.** The path is binary: lurk, or REQUEST A QUOTE
  (high commitment, scary for a cold visitor). There is **no intermediate-commitment
  rung** — no "Save this table," "Request material samples," "Download the dossier,"
  "Book a private viewing." In Hooked terms, *Investment loads the next trigger*: each
  small stored bit of effort (a saved config, a requested sample mailed to their home)
  is both a sunk-cost commitment AND a manufactured future external trigger ("Your
  Paraíba dossier is ready"). Right now there's nothing to load the next session.
- **Tiny thumbnail strip** under the hero = the variable reward (seeing the gemstone
  inlay up close, alternate angles) is under-exploited. Make the visual reveal a small
  unpredictable delight: macro shots of the stone, the table in a real room.
- The "Why" sections are centered and a bit thin/floaty — high motivation copy buried
  in low visual weight. Don't let the most persuasive text be the easiest to skim past.

### 04 — Collection / Atelier (2-band) — **CONVERSION/DESIRE: 6/10**
The "ATELIER / SIGNATURE" split with larger feature tiles up top and a denser
"SIGNATURE" grid below is a reasonable **anchoring → exploration** sequence. The
craftsmanship/atelier framing builds maker-legitimacy (the "Why this house" job, done
visually). But behaviorally this is the **weakest converter** of the four:
- **No CTA momentum.** It's a browsing destination with no clear forward prompt at the
  scroll depth where an inspired buyer is most warm. After the atelier story, there's
  nothing that says "ready? talk to us." Motivation generated here **leaks out** because
  no prompt catches it.
- **Two grids of similar items risk decision fatigue** without a clear "start here."
  More choice ≠ more desire past a point; it dilutes the variable reward into noise.
- Strongest fix: end this page on a single warm prompt ("Start your commission" /
  "Request a viewing") — convert the craftsmanship admiration into an action while the
  feeling is hot.

## Overall — **CONVERSION/DESIRE: 7.5/10**

A genuinely desire-rich, trust-building luxury experience whose **motivation engine is
strong but whose Action/Investment plumbing is thin.** It builds the want beautifully and
then offers the buyer essentially one all-or-nothing door (Request a Quote) with no
lower rungs to climb and nothing stored to pull them back across a weeks-long deliberation.
For a considered purchase, that's the difference between admiration and inquiry.

---

## Top 3 strengths
1. **The hero manufactures genuine desire** — cinematic dark frame + glowing gemstone
   table + identity headline lands an external trigger straight onto the buyer's internal
   one (status/identity/"own the unownable"). Best motivational asset in the set.
2. **Gemstone-naming as an identity/collection mechanic** — "which stone is mine?" is a
   self-relevant variable reward that pulls a deliberating buyer back. Rare and ownable.
3. **PDP "Why this object / house / now" narrative** sequentially defuses the three real
   hesitations of a high-ticket buyer, and "Why now" is honest urgency (lead time /
   scarcity), not a dark pattern. This is textbook ethical persuasion.

## Top 3 concrete behavioral fixes (to increase *qualified* inquiries)
1. **Add an intermediate-commitment rung = Investment that loads the next trigger.**
   Beside every "Request a Quote," add a lower-friction action: **"Request material
   samples" / "Save your table" / "Download the Paraíba dossier" / "Book a private
   viewing."** Each captures a warm-but-not-ready buyer, stores their effort (sunk cost),
   and manufactures a *future external trigger* you control (the sample box arriving, the
   "your saved table" email). This is the highest-leverage change for a weeks-long
   deliberation cycle — it turns one visit into a loop.
2. **Expose price + correct currency everywhere, and make the inquiry CTA omnipresent.**
   Put "From US$X / €X" on collection cards (it qualifies buyers and builds trust — luxury
   hides nothing); render the PDP price in US$/€ for an export-only audience (R$ breaks
   the frame and adds cognitive friction); and add a persistent/sticky "Inquire" so the
   *already-warm* buyer never has to hunt for the door. Reduce Ability cost at the exact
   moment motivation peaks.
3. **Catch the leaking motivation on the Collection & Atelier pages.** End screens 02 and
   04 on a single warm prompt ("Start your commission" / "Request a viewing"), and upgrade
   per-card prompts from generic "View more" to object-named, desire-loaded CTAs ("See
   Paraíba"). Don't let the craftsmanship admiration you generated dissipate with no
   action to catch it.

## #1 friction point in the path to inquiry
**There is exactly one door and it's a heavy one:** every warm visitor faces the single
high-commitment "Request a Quote" with no lower rung to step onto and nothing saved to
pull them back — so a buyer who is interested-but-deliberating (i.e., *most* of this
audience) leaves with their motivation un-captured and no reason to return.

---

### Dark patterns to AVOID (Regret Test — for a luxury brand these *destroy* trust)
- **No fake scarcity / fake countdowns.** "Only 1 left" or expiring timers on a
  made-to-order atelier piece are transparently false and read as cheap — instant luxury
  credibility kill. Keep "Why now" tied to *real* lead times and gemstone availability.
- **No price-gating as a coercion tactic** ("inquire to see price"). Hiding price to force
  contact is friction dressed as exclusivity; this buyer reads it as evasive. Expose it.
- **No guilt/confirm-shaming opt-outs**, no pre-checked consent, no manufactured FOMO
  popups. At this price point the buyer must feel *pursued, never trapped*. Every nudge
  must pass: *would the user thank you if they understood what you did?* If not, cut it.
- **Investment must be voluntary and value-returning** — a saved config or a sample box is
  ethical because the user gets real utility from it; a loop that only benefits Bretda
  fails the Manipulation Matrix. Build loops the buyer would *want*.
