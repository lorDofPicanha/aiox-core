# Dieter Rams — Conclave Critique v1

**Project:** BRETDA — ultra-premium Brazilian atelier, handcrafted luxury game tables, export-only (USA + Europe), prices exposed (US$8k–24k), models named after Brazilian gemstones. Visual direction: dark cinematic, "jewel on dark velvet."

**Lens:** The Ten Principles of Good Design. *Weniger, aber besser.* Good design is as little design as possible. It is honest, unobtrusive, thorough to the last detail. I judge **layout, typography, restraint, hierarchy, composition** — not the placeholder renders.

**Note on method:** I am evaluating the system, not the AI product images (which are placeholders, some sci-fi/neon — irrelevant to my judgment).

---

## A word before the scores

Luxury, properly understood, is not *more*. It is *inevitability*. The object — and the page that presents it — looks as though it could not have been otherwise. Nothing added to impress, nothing removed that was needed. These mockups understand this instinctively. The discipline of the dark canvas, the air around each table, the refusal to shout — this is the correct posture. My criticism is therefore not "do less drama." It is: **the restraint is real but not yet thorough to the last detail.** A few decorative tics and a slightly soft typographic system are the only things standing between "good" and "inevitable."

---

## Screen 01 — Home Hero

**Score: 8 / 10**

The strongest decision here is **what is absent**. A single object, suspended in darkness, a two-line statement, one call to action. That is the correct hierarchy: object first, word second, action third. The thin top navigation knows its place. The "MADE TO ORDER · BRAZIL" eyebrow is honest provenance, quietly stated. Good.

Where it falls short of inevitability:

- **The headline mixes a roman and an italic script ("made to play.") — this is decoration, not structure.** The italic is doing emotional work the object should be doing. An ultra-premium atelier states; it does not flourish. One typeface, one weight relationship, no swash. Let the table be the only thing that performs.
- **"Brazil's gemstones, made to play"** — the copy is charming but slightly clever. Cleverness is a form of insecurity. Honest would be calmer: the gem names *are* the poetry; the line above them should not compete.
- **The CTA button reads as a generic outlined pill.** For a US$8k–24k object, the button is the one moment of friction-by-design. It should feel machined, not web-default. Detail to the last millimetre.

Verdict: This is 80% there. Remove the italic flourish and it climbs immediately.

---

## Screen 02 — Collection (single column / grid)

**Score: 7 / 10**

"The Collection" as a quiet gallery wall is the right idea — each table given its own air, gem name beneath, a restrained "VIEW MORE." This is unobtrusive and understandable. A buyer can read the whole catalogue at a glance. That is good design making the product's structure clear.

Problems of thoroughness:

- **The grid rhythm is irregular** — items appear to alternate alignment/size without a governing reason. Asymmetry is permitted *only* when it serves function (e.g., signalling a flagship). Arbitrary asymmetry is noise. Either a strict, even grid or a deliberate hierarchy with a stated reason — never "varied for visual interest."
- **Prices are absent from the grid.** The brief says all prices are exposed. Honesty (Principle 6) demands the price live *with* the object, even in the index. Hiding it until the PDP makes the product "appear more than it is" — exactly what I warn against. A quiet "From US$11,400" under each name is more confident, not less.
- **Repeated "VIEW MORE" on every card is redundant** — the whole card is the affordance. Remove the label; let the image and name be the link. Less but better.

Verdict: Sound bones, but the irregular grid and the missing prices are honesty/thoroughness failures.

---

## Screen 03 — PDP (Paraíba)

**Score: 7.5 / 10**

The product-detail page is the most *honest* screen and I respect it. Gallery left, name + "From R$24,500" + a clear primary action right. Then the "Why this object / Why this house / Why now" sections give reasons. Reasons are the foundation of trust at this price. This is the page doing real work.

But here the decoration creeps back in:

- **The three small ornamental glyphs/dividers between sections (the diamond/marks) are pure ornament.** They are not structure; they are jewellery on the page imitating the jewellery of the product. Delete them. Whitespace divides sections more honestly than a decorative mark ever will.
- **"Why this object / Why this house / Why now"** — three centred headings, centred body text. Centred long-form text is harder to read and feels ceremonial rather than confident. Left-align the prose; reserve centring for the single object, not paragraphs.
- **CRITICAL HONESTY FLAW: the price shows "R$24,500" — Brazilian reais.** The brief states export-only, English site, multi-currency $/€ by geo. A USA buyer seeing R$ is a broken promise and a trust rupture at the exact moment of decision. This is not a style note; it is a functional defect. The currency must resolve to the visitor's geography.
- The secondary actions (download / configure / etc.) are well-restrained — they don't fight the primary CTA. Good.

Verdict: Functionally the best page; let down by ornamental glyphs, centred prose, and a currency that contradicts the business.

---

## Screen 04 — Collection / Atelier (2-band layout)

**Score: 7 / 10**

The two-band structure — a sparse "ATELIER" feature band of large objects above, a denser "SIGNATURE" grid below — is a legitimate hierarchy: hero pieces, then the full range. When asymmetry has a *reason*, I permit it, and here it nearly earns it.

Where it wavers:

- **The relationship between the two bands is not yet clearly stated.** Why is a table in ATELIER and not SIGNATURE? If the distinction is real (flagship vs. range, or bespoke vs. catalogue), say it in one honest line. If it is merely "big images look nice up top," it is arbitrary and must go.
- **Two competing section headings ("ATELIER", "SIGNATURE") in the same scroll** risk diluting the brand voice. One organising idea per page. Decide whether this page is *the collection* or *the atelier story* — it is currently trying to be both.
- The lower grid is tighter and more even than Screen 02's — **this is the better grid; make Screen 02 match it.**
- Again: **names without prices.** Same honesty note as Screen 02.

Verdict: The most architecturally ambitious page; needs one clear, stated reason for its two-band split, or it should collapse into the cleaner single system.

---

## Overall

**Score: 7.5 / 10**

This is genuinely good design — restrained, dark, object-first, unafraid of silence. It is *most* of the way to the quiet inevitability that real luxury possesses. It is not "trying too hard"; if anything it occasionally reaches for a flourish (the italic, the glyphs) precisely when it should trust the object and the void. The failures are not of taste but of **thoroughness (Principle 8) and honesty (Principle 6)**: an irregular grid, hidden prices, a wrong-currency price, decorative dividers, and a slightly performative type system.

Remove the decoration, fix the honesty, tighten the system to a single ruthless grid and one typographic logic — and this stops looking *designed* and starts looking *inevitable*. That is the whole game.

---

## Top 3 Strengths
1. **Object-first restraint.** Each table is given darkness and air; the page never competes with the product. This is the correct luxury posture.
2. **Honest information architecture on the PDP.** "Why this object / house / now" gives real reasons — trust earned, not asserted.
3. **Confident use of the void.** The dark canvas is used as a material, not a backdrop. Silence is doing the work.

## Top 3 Concrete Fixes
1. **Fix the currency + expose prices everywhere.** PDP must show $/€ by geo (never R$ to an export buyer), and the collection grids must carry a quiet "From US$X" under each gem name. Honesty, Principle 6.
2. **Delete the decoration.** Remove the italic "made to play." flourish (Screen 01) and the ornamental section glyphs (Screen 03). Let whitespace and the object carry the emotion.
3. **One ruthless grid + left-aligned prose.** Adopt Screen 04's tighter even grid across the whole collection; kill arbitrary asymmetry; left-align all body copy and reserve centring for the single object alone.

## The single change: good → truly spectacular
**Impose one typographic logic and one grid with absolute discipline — a single typeface family, one weight relationship, no italics or flourishes, on one even grid — so that nothing on the page is arbitrary; then the silence around each gemstone becomes the only voice, and that quiet is what reads as spectacular.**
