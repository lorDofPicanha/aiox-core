# Tobias van Schneider — Bretda Website v2 (stitch-v1) Critique

*Consultation through authentic Voice DNA. Lens: bold editorial type, cinematic art direction, emotion over convention, anti-template, premium-in-the-details. Note: table images are AI placeholders — I am judging art direction, type, layout, emotion, NOT render quality.*

---

## The honest one-liner

This is a **good dark theme**. It is not yet a **world**. Right now it reads like a competent agency template that got the memo "make it dark and luxury" — velvet background, gold accents, serif headline, centered everything. Every premium furniture/watch brand on Squarespace looks roughly like this. For a US$8k–24k atelier where each table is named after a gemstone, the design has to have a **point of view someone would argue with**. This one is polite. Polite is the enemy of unforgettable.

The good news: the *raw material* is exceptional. A jewel-toned Paraíba tourmaline table glowing on near-black is one of the most cinematic product setups I have seen for this category. The problem is the layout is treating a film still like a stock photo.

## TOP 3 STRENGTHS

1. **The jewel-on-velvet art direction is the whole brand in one frame.** That glowing Paraíba on near-black is your "color you own." Hold onto it like a religion — it is the single most ownable thing here.
2. **"Why this object / Why this house / Why now" is a real editorial spine.** That is luxury thinking — selling a worldview, not a spec sheet. Most furniture sites would list dimensions. This reaches for narrative. Keep it; it just needs to be *staged* like a story instead of stacked like a FAQ.
3. **Restraint in palette is correct.** Near-black + warm gold + the gem accent is a disciplined, ownable system. You constrained the palette aggressively — that part you nailed. Now earn the right to be bold inside that constraint.

---

## TOP 3 CONCRETE FIXES

**1. Break the symmetry — kill the centered card grid (Screen 02 is the priority).**
The collection grid is the weakest moment and the most-visited page after the hero. A centered, evenly-weighted 2×N grid says "catalog," not "atelier." Redesign it as an **editorial spread**: one hero gemstone per viewport at near-full-bleed, asymmetric — image bleeding off one edge, the gemstone name set huge and tight against the opposite margin, the next piece offset so the eye *travels* diagonally down the page. Vary the scale: Paraíba (the star) gets a full screen; lesser models share a band. Hierarchy = story. Right now every table is equally important, which means none of them is.

**2. Make the type *brave*. The headline must be a poster, not a caption.**
"Brazil's gemstones, made to play" set in a tasteful mid-size italic serif is a magazine *dek*, not a hero. Push the type scale to genuinely cinematic — display serif at 8–14vw, set tight (negative tracking, line-height ~0.9), one word per line if needed, so the words have the same physical weight as the table. Let the headline **overlap and interlock with the product** (type behind/in front of the table edge) so image and word are one composition, not two stacked layers. And drop the mixed roman/italic cuteness — commit to one brave cut. (Typography refinement → hand the final cut to @erik-spiekermann, but the *ambition* is set here: editorial, oversized, fearless.)

**3. Redesign the PDP price/CTA zone so it doesn't break the spell.**
On Screen 03 the "From R$24,500 / Request a quote" block is the only part that looks like software. At this price point the commerce furniture should feel *engraved*, not transactional: price set in the editorial serif (not a UI label), spaced and quiet; the CTA a thin hairline-bordered word ("Inquire") with generous letter-spacing rather than a filled button. And **multi-currency ($/€) is a luxury signal — surface it elegantly**, not as a dev dropdown. Premium lives in exactly these micro-details most people won't consciously notice — but they *feel* the cheapness.

*(Bonus, free of charge: every screen is centered. Centered everything is the typographic equivalent of a shrug. Pick a strong left margin as your editorial baseline and let the asymmetry create tension.)*

## MOTION & ART DIRECTION — where I'd push it to unforgettable

- **The hero should breathe like a film, not load like a webpage.** Slow, subtle parallax on the table; the gem's glow should *pulse* almost imperceptibly (1–2% luminance, 6s ease) so the jewel feels alive. Headline words rise in on a staggered, weighted ease — like a title card, not a fade-in.
- **Gemstone as the transition device.** Navigating between models, let the accent color *morph* — Paraíba's electric teal bleeds into Esmeralda's green into Ametista's violet. The whole site's lighting temperature shifts with the stone you're looking at. That is a brand mechanic no competitor can copy, because it's born from *your* naming concept.
- **Editorial rhythm = tempo changes.** Right now every section has the same pacing (centered block, breathe, centered block). A magazine accelerates and slows: a silent full-bleed cinematic moment, then a dense tight-set detail spread, then white (black) space that lets one line of type hang alone. Vary the tempo and the site gains a heartbeat.

---

## THE SINGLE BOLDEST MOVE

**Stop designing pages and design a gem-cutter's lookbook: one model per full screen, the gemstone name set in oversized display serif that physically interlocks with its glowing table, the entire site's color temperature shifting stone-by-stone — so scrolling Bretda feels like opening a jeweler's velvet trays one at a time, not browsing a furniture catalog.**
